07 / Questions

How does the CPD unit work?

NSW runs on self-assessment. There's no accreditation body to approve a session - the Legal Profession Uniform CPD (Solicitors) Rules 2015 set the tests for what counts, and this hour is built against them. One hour of activity is one CPD unit. You self-assess the field; most attendees put it under practice management and business skills or professional skills, and I hand everyone the paperwork for their records.

Is it accredited?

No, because nothing is in NSW. Solicitor CPD has no provider accreditation - self-assessment is the system, not a workaround.

What does it cost?

Nothing. I'm building my name with law firms on the Beaches and I'd rather do that by being useful first. If the hour is worth anything, you'll know who to call later.

Is it a sales pitch?

No. No product demos, no slides about my services, nothing for sale in the room. The hour is the product.

Are you a lawyer?

No, and I don't pretend to be. I build the systems around legal practice - intake, document flow, admin - and I stay in that lane. Nothing in the session is legal advice.

Do we need to prepare anything?

A room, a screen and the hour. I don't touch your systems or your files, and nothing confidential is used or discussed.

Who should be in the room?

Anyone whose week the admin eats. Solicitors claim the CPD unit; practice managers and support staff are welcome and usually get the most out of the time-mapping part.

What if lunch or 5pm doesn't work?

Then pick a time that does. Those two just tend to be when a firm can get everyone in one room.
