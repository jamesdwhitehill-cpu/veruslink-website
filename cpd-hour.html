One hour. One CPD unit. At your firm.

I'm James Whitehill. I build the systems around legal practice - the intake, the document flow, the admin drag - never the legal work itself. This is a standing offer to law firms on the Northern Beaches: I'll come to your office, lunchtime or 5pm, and give your solicitors one hour called AI in a Law Firm: What Good Looks Like.

What the hour covers

Lawyers weigh two things: risk and time. The hour is built on both.

Where AI actually sits in a firm. What the tools really are, what they're good and bad at, and the shadow-AI problem: when a firm bans AI instead of providing a safe version, people use it in secret, on client work, where you can't see it.

What good looks like. The three questions that tell you in five minutes whether any tool is safe near a client file, and the standard a firm should hold every tool to: it asks before it acts, a person signs off, and you can always show who was accountable.

The traps. Why your name on the document means you wrote it, whatever drafted it. Why confidentiality and privilege are not the same thing.

Where your time is best spent. The admin hours no client pays for - intake, chasing signatures, rekeying, status updates - and which of them systems can win back without adding risk.

Everyone leaves with the three-questions test card and a one-page priority card for their own practice.

The CPD part

The session is designed to satisfy the requirements of the Legal Profession Uniform Continuing Professional Development (Solicitors) Rules 2015. Attendees may claim 1 CPD unit for the hour. Under the Rules, solicitors self-assess the eligibility and field of CPD activities - most attendees will find it fits practice management and business skills or professional skills.

Every attendee receives the paperwork for their CPD records: the program with timings, the learning outcomes, my presenter statement, and a certificate of attendance.

What it isn't

It isn't legal advice, and I'm not a lawyer - I present from the systems seat and stay there. It isn't a product demo. Nothing is sold in the room, and there's no charge for the hour. If it's useful, you'll know who to call. That's the whole model.

What you need

A room, a screen, and an hour. Lunchtime or 5pm, whichever suits the firm. I bring everything else.
